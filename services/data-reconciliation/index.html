RECONCILIATION SCENARIOS

Where discrepancies arise

Database migrations, ledger synchronizations, payment provider reconciliations, and reporting feed comparisons all require proving that two separate data representations agree.

A successful reconciliation does not mean ignoring differences. It means cataloguing every mismatch with mathematical precision, isolating data transformations from genuine errors, and providing an auditable proof of state.

OUR METHODOLOGY

The 7-step reconciliation process

How we turn cross-system uncertainty into an auditable, repeatable operation.

1. Understand

Identify participating source and target systems, underlying business entities, volume expectations, and core reconciliation objectives.

2. Map

Define explicit data mapping specifications, documenting exactly how source fields, structures, and enumerations translate to target schema equivalents.

3. Match

Establish primary and composite keys, normalization rules, numeric tolerance thresholds (currency rounding), and timing cut-off windows.

4. Reconcile

Compare datasets deterministically using automated, client-perimeter tooling built for large extracts, without cloud data egress.

5. Investigate

Systematically classify discrepancies: missing records, unexpected extra rows, transformed values, floating-point variances, or cut-off anomalies.

6. Automate

Package validated matching configurations into repeatable pipelines for continuous integration, regression testing, or recurring ledger balancing.

7. Evidence

Produce auditable reports and exception manifests that business stakeholders, compliance teams, and technical owners can inspect with confidence.

1. The Source & Mapping Contract

Source: Core Banking Trading OLTP (Postgres / SQL extract).
Target: General Ledger & Regulatory Reporting Store (Snowflake / Analytical Warehouse).

• Primary Reconciliation Key: TRX_ID → TRANSACTION_REF

3. Discrepancy Analysis & Diagnostic Explanations

Instead of vague error alerts, our reconciliation workflow isolates each exception to its root engineering cause with actionable remediation:

Mismatch #1: Precision Truncation vs Banker's Rounding (TRX-9402)

Mechanism & Explanation: The target ETL pipeline used integer bitwise truncation (CAST AS NUMERIC(18,2) in legacy SQL without explicit rounding mode), stripping the third decimal instead of applying half-up rounding. Over millions of daily micro-transactions, this produces systematic unbalancing in downstream financial general ledgers.
Fix: Enforce deterministic banker's rounding (ROUND_HALF_EVEN) in the ingestion contract before target persistence.

Mismatch #2: Boundary Cut-Off Timing Asynchrony (TRX-9403)

Mechanism & Explanation: Source executed trade at 23:59:58 UTC. Due to a 4-second replication queue latency across the message broker, the target ingest pipeline stamped arrival at 00:00:02 UTC and partitioned the record into Day T+1. The trade was not lost, but Day T balance showed a £350,000 shortfall and Day T+1 showed a matching surplus.
Fix: Bind reconciliation windows to the source transaction execution timestamp rather than consumer pipeline arrival timestamp.

Mismatch #3: Unmapped Enumeration / Schema Drift Rejection (TRX-9404)

Mechanism & Explanation: The legacy source introduced a variant instrument code FX_FWD_ND that was absent from the target warehouse lookup dictionary. The strict target schema rejected the entire row into a dead-letter queue without throwing a fatal pipeline exception, leaving the target ledger silently short CHF 15,200.
Fix: Update reference mapping enum dictionary and add automated dead-letter queue intake validation alerts.

SPECIALIZED PRACTICE

Migration Assurance

Prove your data survives migration and transformation intact.

When cutting over between core platforms, databases, or warehouse layers, migration teams need independent, automated verification that records arrived completely, accurately, and without silent corruption.

Core Migration Assurance deliverables:

  • Completeness & Volume Proofs: Verifying row counts and entity coverage across all migrated partitions.
  • Pre/Post Cutover Comparisons: Fast, deterministic reconciliation during dry-runs and live go-live cutover windows.
  • Transformation Integrity: Auditing complex business logic transitions to verify calculations match historical baselines.
  • Cutover Sign-Off Reports: Defensible documentation for steering committees, risk officers, and auditors.
